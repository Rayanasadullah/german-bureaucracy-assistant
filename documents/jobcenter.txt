JOBCENTER AND EMPLOYMENT OFFICE IN GERMANY
Source: Bundesagentur für Arbeit (Federal Employment Agency), BAMF (2025-2026)

WHAT IS THE JOBCENTER?
The Jobcenter is a German government office that helps people who are unemployed or struggling financially to find work and provides financial support. There are two separate systems in Germany for unemployment:

1. ARBEITSAGENTUR (Employment Agency / Agentur für Arbeit) — for people who were previously employed and paid into the unemployment insurance system.
2. JOBCENTER — for people receiving Bürgergeld (citizen's income / social welfare), including long-term unemployed, people with no work history, and people who need additional support.

UNEMPLOYMENT BENEFIT I (ARBEITSLOSENGELD I / ALG I):
- For people who were employed and paid into unemployment insurance (Arbeitslosenversicherung).
- You must have worked and contributed for at least 12 months in the past 2 years.
- You must register as unemployed at the Arbeitsagentur (Employment Agency) on the FIRST day you become unemployed.
- Benefit amount: approximately 60% of your previous net salary (67% if you have children).
- Duration: typically 12 months (up to 24 months if you are over 58 years old and worked longer).
- Paid by the Arbeitsagentur (NOT the Jobcenter).

HOW TO REGISTER AS UNEMPLOYED (Arbeitslos melden):
1. Register at your local Arbeitsagentur office as soon as possible — ideally at least 3 months before your job ends (called Frühzeitig melden / early notification).
2. Bring: passport/ID, work contract, dismissal letter, residence permit (if non-EU), social security number (Sozialversicherungsnummer), bank account details.
3. You must be actively looking for work.
4. You must accept reasonable job offers. Refusing without good reason may reduce your benefits.

BÜRGERGELD (Citizens' Income — replaces Hartz IV since January 2023):
- For people who are long-term unemployed, or whose ALG I has run out, or who have never worked.
- Also for people who work but earn too little to cover living expenses.
- Applied for at the local Jobcenter (not the Arbeitsagentur).
- Bürgergeld covers: basic living costs, rent and heating costs, social and health insurance.
- Standard rate (2026): approx. €563/month for single adults (adjusted annually).
- Children also receive Bürgergeld and additional child support.

WHO CAN APPLY FOR BÜRGERGELD?
- German citizens and people with valid residence permits in Germany.
- People must be able and willing to work (between 15 and 65 years old, not in full-time education).
- You and your partner/household must not have significant savings above the exemption threshold.
- EU citizens can apply, but conditions apply (must have worked in Germany or have right of residence based on work).
- Non-EU nationals must have a residence permit that allows work.

HOW TO APPLY FOR BÜRGERGELD:
1. Contact your local Jobcenter (find it at jobcenter.digital).
2. Submit an application form with required documents.
3. Required documents: ID/passport, residence permit, bank statements, rental contract, health insurance details, details of savings/assets.
4. The Jobcenter will assess your needs and create an Integration Agreement (Eingliederungsvereinbarung) — a plan outlining your job search responsibilities.

IMPORTANT: As a recipient you must:
- Actively search for work and document your job applications.
- Attend all appointments at the Jobcenter.
- Accept reasonable job offers.
- Inform the Jobcenter of any changes in income or living situation.
- Failure to comply can result in benefit sanctions (reduction of payments).

JOB SEARCH SUPPORT:
The Jobcenter and Arbeitsagentur offer:
- Job placement assistance (Arbeitsvermittlung).
- Vocational training and retraining support.
- Language course funding (German language courses for immigrants).
- Integration courses (Integrationskurs) — the Jobcenter can refer you or fund attendance.
- Subsidised childcare support.
- CV and application coaching.

INTEGRATION COURSE (INTEGRATIONSKURS):
The integration course is a German language and civic knowledge course offered to immigrants. It consists of:
- Up to 700 hours of German language classes (reaching B1 level).
- 100 hours of orientation course (German law, history, culture).
It is often mandatory for certain visa and residence permit types. The Jobcenter or BAMF can register you. Costs are subsidised. Contact BAMF or your local Jobcenter/Arbeitsagentur.

WORK PERMIT AND JOBCENTER:
Non-EU nationals need a residence permit that allows work before they can take a job. The Jobcenter can advise, but cannot grant a work permit — that is done by the Ausländerbehörde (Foreigners' Office).

FINDING YOUR LOCAL JOBCENTER OR ARBEITSAGENTUR:
- Online: jobcenter.digital (for Jobcenter)
- Online: arbeitsagentur.de (for Arbeitsagentur / Employment Agency)
- In person: offices in every city and district in Germany.
