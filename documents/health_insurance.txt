HEALTH INSURANCE IN GERMANY (KRANKENVERSICHERUNG)
Source: iamexpat.de (updated April 2025), GKV-Spitzenverband

OVERVIEW:
Health insurance is MANDATORY in Germany for everyone. You must have either statutory (public) or private health insurance. Having no health insurance is illegal, and back payments can be charged for your entire uninsured period.

TWO TYPES OF HEALTH INSURANCE:

1. STATUTORY HEALTH INSURANCE (Gesetzliche Krankenversicherung — GKV):
- Covers most people in Germany.
- If you work and earn LESS than €73,800/year (2025), you are automatically enrolled.
- Contributions: approximately 14.6% of gross salary, split equally between employer and employee, plus a supplementary contribution (average ~1.7%) set by each insurer.
- Covers: doctor visits, hospital stays, basic dental care, prescriptions, maternity care.
- Your spouse and children can often be covered for free under family insurance (Familienversicherung) if they have no income.
- Also provides: accident insurance, long-term care insurance (Pflegeversicherung), sickness benefit (Krankengeld), maternity benefit.

Major statutory health insurers (Krankenkassen) accepting expats in English:
- TK (Techniker Krankenkasse): signuptk.de
- BARMER: signupbarmer.de
- AOK (regional public insurer)
- SBK (Siemens-Betriebskrankenkasse)

2. PRIVATE HEALTH INSURANCE (Private Krankenversicherung — PKV):
- For self-employed, freelancers, and high earners (over €73,800/year or €6,150/month in 2025).
- About 10% of Germany's population has private insurance.
- Generally offers: shorter wait times, more comprehensive cover, private hospital rooms, better dental coverage, access to senior doctors.
- Contributions are risk-based (based on age and health, NOT income).
- Can be harder to switch back to statutory insurance once you leave.
- Providers for expats: Ottonova, Feather, Hallesche, Getsafe, DKV, AXA.

DO I NEED GERMAN HEALTH INSURANCE?
- EU citizens: Covered temporarily by European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) for up to 90 days. After registering (Anmeldung), you must get German health insurance.
- Non-EU citizens: German health insurance is usually required to get a residence permit. Foreign insurance is generally not accepted for residence permit applications.
- Students: Must have health insurance before enrolling at a German university. Some foreign health insurance policies are accepted — check with your insurer and university.

IMPORTANT: You need proof of health insurance for:
- Applying for a residence permit (Aufenthaltstitel).
- Enrolling at a German university.
- Renewing your residence permit.

HOW TO GET HEALTH INSURANCE:
As an employee: Your employer registers you with a statutory insurer. You can choose which Krankenkasse you want.
As a self-employed person / freelancer: Apply directly to a private insurer or voluntarily join a statutory insurer.
As a student: Contact TK, BARMER, or AOK directly for student health insurance.
As someone looking for work: If you receive Bürgergeld (unemployment benefit), the Jobcenter covers your health insurance contributions.

FAMILY INSURANCE (Familienversicherung):
If you have statutory health insurance, your spouse and children can be covered for free under family insurance if:
- Your spouse earns less than €505/month (2025), or less than €520/month if they have a mini-job.
- Your children are under 25 (if in education or vocational training).

SICKNESS BENEFIT (Krankengeld):
If you are employed and sick for more than 6 weeks, your employer stops paying your salary. At that point, your statutory health insurer pays sickness benefit: 70% of your previous gross salary (but no more than 90% of your net salary).

LONG-TERM CARE INSURANCE (Pflegeversicherung):
Automatically included with statutory health insurance. Covers costs if you need long-term care (nursing home, home care). Contribution: 3.4% of gross salary split between employer and employee (higher if you have no children).

CHANGING YOUR HEALTH INSURER:
You can switch statutory insurers once a year with 2 months' notice. Compare contribution rates (Zusatzbeitrag) between insurers — most cover the same services, so switching to a cheaper one can save money.

PENALTIES FOR NOT HAVING INSURANCE:
Failure to have health insurance is illegal. If you are found to have been uninsured, you can be required to pay back contributions for the entire period you were uninsured. There is no way to avoid paying for past uninsured periods.
