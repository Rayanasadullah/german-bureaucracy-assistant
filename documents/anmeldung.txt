ANMELDUNG - REGISTERING YOUR ADDRESS IN GERMANY
Source: iamexpat.de (updated 2025), Federal Office for Migration and Refugees (BAMF)

WHAT IS THE ANMELDUNG?
Anmeldung means registering your home address at your local citizens' office (Bürgeramt or Einwohnermeldeamt). This is compulsory for everyone living in Germany — both Germans and foreigners. You must register within 14 days of moving into a new apartment.

WHY DO YOU NEED TO REGISTER?
- You receive your registration certificate (Anmeldebestätigung), which is needed for almost everything in Germany.
- Your Tax ID (Steuerliche Identifikationsnummer) is automatically sent to you by post after registration (takes 2-3 weeks).
- You need it to apply for a residence permit (Aufenthaltstitel).
- You need it to open a bank account.
- You need it to get health insurance.
- You need it to enrol at a university or school.
- Without registration, you cannot do most official tasks in Germany.

WHO NEEDS TO REGISTER?
Everyone who stays in Germany for more than 3 months must register. Even if you are staying for less than 3 months but will be working or studying, you still need to register. EU citizens, non-EU citizens, and Germans all must register.

BEFORE YOU REGISTER - IMPORTANT:
You need a Wohnungsgeberbestätigung (landlord confirmation / proof of residence certificate). Your landlord is legally required to give you this document. Without it, you cannot register. If your landlord refuses, it may be an illegal sublet — avoid such apartments.

DOCUMENTS NEEDED FOR ANMELDUNG:
1. Valid passport or ID card (not a driving licence)
2. Visa (if you have one)
3. Wohnungsgeberbestätigung (landlord proof of residence — must be signed by landlord)
4. Completed registration form (Anmeldeformular) — can be downloaded from your city's website
5. Marriage certificate (if registering with spouse)
6. Child's birth certificate (if registering children)
Note: Documents not in German or English may need a certified translation.

HOW TO REGISTER (IN PERSON):
1. Book an appointment (Termin) at your local Bürgeramt / Einwohnermeldeamt.
   - Berlin: service.berlin.de
   - Munich: stadt.muenchen.de
   - Hamburg: serviceportal.hamburg.de
   - Frankfurt: frankfurt.de/buergeraemter
   - Cologne: termine.stadt-koeln.de
   - For other cities: search "Anmeldung Termin + [your city]"
2. Go to the appointment with all documents.
3. The official checks your documents and fills in the paperwork.
4. You receive your stamped Anmeldebestätigung (registration certificate) immediately.
5. Your Tax ID arrives by post within 2-3 weeks.
The appointment usually lasts 10-15 minutes. Staff may not speak English, so bring a German speaker if possible. You can also send someone with a written authorisation (Vollmacht) on your behalf.

IMPORTANT NOTE ON APPOINTMENTS:
Appointments can be hard to get, especially in big cities. The 14-day deadline is rarely strictly enforced. Keep checking the website regularly as new slots appear. Some offices offer drop-in times.

CHURCH TAX WARNING:
At registration you will be asked your religion. If you say Protestant (evangelisch) or Catholic (katholisch), you will automatically pay church tax (8-9% of income tax). To avoid this, state "ohne Religion" (no religion).

CHANGING ADDRESS (UMMELDUNG):
If you move within Germany, you must re-register your new address within 14 days. You do not need to deregister from your old address — it happens automatically. You may be able to do this online in some cities if you already have a German eID.

DEREGISTERING (ABMELDUNG):
When you permanently leave Germany, you must deregister within 14 days of moving out. You receive an Abmeldebescheinigung (deregistration certificate), which you need to cancel health insurance, cancel contracts, stop paying TV tax (Rundfunkbeitrag), and potentially get pension contributions refunded.

FINE FOR NOT REGISTERING:
Failing to register, registering late, or giving incorrect details can result in a fine of up to €1,000.
