RESIDENCE PERMIT IN GERMANY (AUFENTHALTSTITEL / AUFENTHALTSERLAUBNIS)
Source: iamexpat.de (updated Dec 2025), BAMF

WHAT IS A RESIDENCE PERMIT?
A residence permit (Aufenthaltstitel) gives non-EU nationals the right to live and work in Germany. It is issued as a plastic credit card with a chip — called the electronic Residence Title (eAT - elektronischer Aufenthaltstitel). Paper titles are no longer valid since August 2021.

WHO NEEDS ONE?
- EU/EEA citizens: Do NOT need a residence permit. They can live and work freely. But must still do Anmeldung if staying more than 3 months.
- Swiss citizens: Need a special declaratory residence permit from the local Foreigners' Office (Ausländerbehörde).
- Non-EU/EEA citizens staying more than 90 days: NEED a residence permit.
- Citizens of Australia, Canada, UK, USA, Japan, South Korea, New Zealand, Israel can enter Germany without a visa and apply for a residence permit from inside Germany.
- All other non-EU nationals need a national visa first, then convert it to a residence permit after arrival.

WHERE TO APPLY:
Apply at your local Foreigners' Office (Ausländerbehörde). Find yours at: bamf.de (search Ausländerbehörde locator).

TYPES OF TEMPORARY RESIDENCE PERMITS (AUFENTHALTSERLAUBNIS):

1. WORK PERMIT (§ 18 Aufenthaltsgesetz)
- For people with a job offer in Germany.
- Valid up to 4 years, renewable.
- Requires: recognised qualifications, job offer/contract, professional practice permit (for regulated professions).
- For people over 45: gross annual salary must be at least €55,770 (2026).

2. EU BLUE CARD (§ 18g)
- For highly-skilled workers from outside the EU.
- Requires: university degree + salary of at least €50,700/year (2026), or €45,934 for shortage occupations (IT, medicine, engineering, maths).
- Valid up to 4 years.
- After 27 months → can apply for permanent residence (or 21 months with B1 German).
- Spouse can come without German language requirement.

3. STUDY PERMIT (§ 16b)
- For full-time students at state-recognised German universities.
- Valid 2 years (or length of course), renewable.
- Can work up to 140 days/year (or 280 half-days).
- After graduation: 18-month extension to look for work.

4. OPPORTUNITY CARD (CHANCENKARTE) — Available since 2024
- For non-EU nationals to come to Germany and look for a job for up to 12 months.
- Can work up to 20 hours/week during this period.
- Two pathways: skilled professional pathway or points-based system.
- Points awarded for: qualifications, German/English language skills, age (under 35 = 2 pts), previous Germany stays, etc. Minimum 6 points required for points-based pathway.
- When you find work, convert to a work residence permit.

5. FAMILY REUNIFICATION
- Spouse/children can join a permit holder.
- Spouse needs basic German skills (exception for Blue Card holders and highly qualified).
- Children must be under 16 and unmarried to join under family reunification.

6. SELF-EMPLOYMENT (§ 21)
- For entrepreneurs. Valid up to 3 years.
- Must show economic interest, adequate capital, and business plan.
- After 3 successful years → can apply for permanent settlement permit.

7. VOCATIONAL TRAINING (§ 16a)
- For people doing vocational education or apprenticeships in Germany.
- Can work up to 20 hours/week alongside training.
- 18-month extension after completing training to look for work.

STEPS BEFORE APPLYING FOR A RESIDENCE PERMIT:
1. Do your Anmeldung (city registration) — you need the registration certificate.
2. Get German health insurance — foreign insurance usually not accepted.
3. Open a German bank account to prove financial means.
4. If no income: open a blocked account (Sperrkonto) as proof of funds.

REQUIRED DOCUMENTS (general):
- Valid passport
- 1 biometric passport photo
- Completed application form (available from your local Ausländerbehörde)
- Registration certificate (Anmeldebestätigung / Meldebescheinigung)
- Proof of health insurance
- Proof of financial means (bank statements, employment contract, etc.)
- Proof of purpose: employment contract / enrolment letter / business plan, etc.

FEES:
- Application fee: €50–€110 (less for minors)
- Most offices accept EC cards, not credit cards or cash.
- Fees are non-refundable if application is rejected.

PROCESSING TIME:
2–4 weeks (can be longer in busy periods). While waiting, you are covered by a Fiktionsbescheinigung (fictional certificate) proving you are in Germany legally.

FIKTIONSBESCHEINIGUNG:
A temporary document proving legal residence while your application is being processed. Costs ~€15. Apply for it at the same time as your residence permit application.

COLLECTING YOUR PERMIT:
You must collect your eAT card in person at the Foreigners' Office. You may also receive a PIN code by post, which you need to collect the permit.

RENEWING YOUR RESIDENCE PERMIT:
- Most temporary permits are valid for 1 year.
- The office will NOT automatically notify you of expiry — check the date yourself!
- Apply for renewal well before expiry (expiring without renewal can result in a ban from Germany).
- Renewal fee: €50–€80.

PERMANENT RESIDENCE PERMIT (NIEDERLASSUNGSERLAUBNIS):
- Apply after 5 years of legal residence in Germany on a temporary permit.
- Requirements: self-sufficient income (no welfare), 60 months of pension contributions, B1 German, clean criminal record, adequate housing, permission to be employed.
- EU Blue Card holders: eligible after 27 months (21 months with B1 German).
- Skilled workers: after 3 years.
- Graduates of German universities: after 2 years.
- Fee: approximately €113 (general), €124 (self-employed), €147 (highly qualified).

EU LONG-TERM RESIDENCE PERMIT (ERLAUBNIS ZUM DAUERAUFENTHALT-EU):
- Similar to the settlement permit but also gives freedom of movement across all EU member states.

If permanent residence expires: Leaving Germany for more than 6 months (or 12 months for EU permit outside EU) causes it to expire.
