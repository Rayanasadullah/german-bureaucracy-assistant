ID CARDS AND ELECTRONIC RESIDENCE TITLE (eAT) IN GERMANY
Source: BAMF, Bundesdruckerei, iamexpat.de (2025-2026)

GERMAN ID CARD (PERSONALAUSWEIS) — FOR GERMAN CITIZENS AND EU NATIONALS:
The German ID card (Personalausweis) is for German citizens only. EU/EEA citizens can use their home country's national ID card or passport to prove their identity in Germany. Germany accepts valid EU national ID cards for most administrative purposes.

ELECTRONIC RESIDENCE TITLE (eAT — ELEKTRONISCHER AUFENTHALTSTITEL) — FOR NON-EU NATIONALS:
Since September 2011, all residence permits for non-EU nationals in Germany are issued as a plastic credit card with an embedded chip. This is called the elektronischer Aufenthaltstitel (eAT). Paper residence titles are no longer valid as of August 31, 2021.

WHAT DOES THE eAT LOOK LIKE?
The eAT card looks like a credit card. It contains:
- Your full name and date of birth.
- Your nationality.
- Your passport photo.
- Your fingerprints (stored on the chip).
- The type of residence permit you hold (e.g. work permit, study permit, EU Blue Card).
- The validity date of your permit.
- Any restrictions or conditions on your permit (e.g. work authorization).
- An online identification function (eID).

eID FUNCTION ON THE eAT:
The chip on your eAT card can be used for secure online identification. This is called the eID function. You can use it to:
- Prove your identity online with German government services.
- Access some online administrative processes (e.g. online Ummeldung in some cities).
- Sign documents electronically.
To use the eID function, you need:
- An NFC-capable smartphone or a card reader.
- The AusweisApp (free app from the official German government).
- Your 6-digit PIN (sent separately by post when you collect your eAT).

HOW TO GET YOUR eAT:
1. Apply for your residence permit at your local Foreigners' Office (Ausländerbehörde).
2. Bring all required documents (passport, registration certificate, health insurance, proof of finances, etc.).
3. Your biometric data (photo and fingerprints) is recorded at the office.
4. The eAT is produced by the Bundesdruckerei (Federal Printing Office) — takes 2-3 weeks.
5. You will receive a notification to collect your eAT card in person at the Foreigners' Office.
6. Your PIN code is sent separately by post. You need this to activate the eID function.

IMPORTANT: You must pick up your eAT card IN PERSON. Someone else cannot collect it for you.

WHILE WAITING FOR YOUR eAT:
You will receive a Fiktionsbescheinigung (fictional certificate) — a temporary paper document proving you are in Germany legally while your eAT is being produced. This allows you to continue working and living in Germany.

WHAT INFORMATION IS ON THE BACK OF THE eAT?
The back of the card shows the conditions of your permit, for example:
- "Erwerbstätigkeit erlaubt" = Employment allowed (you can work freely)
- "Beschäftigung nur mit Genehmigung der Ausländerbehörde" = Employment only with permission of the Foreigners' Office
- "Selbständige Tätigkeit nicht gestattet" = Self-employment not permitted
Always read the back of your card carefully to know what you are allowed to do!

RENEWING YOUR eAT:
- Your eAT expires when your residence permit expires.
- The Foreigners' Office does NOT automatically remind you.
- Apply for renewal well before expiry — at least 6-8 weeks before.
- You must apply in person at the Foreigners' Office (Ausländerbehörde).
- Bring your current eAT, valid passport, registration certificate, and proof that your situation is unchanged (employment contract, health insurance, etc.).

LOST OR STOLEN eAT:
If your eAT is lost or stolen:
1. Report it to the police immediately and get a police report (Anzeige).
2. Contact your local Foreigners' Office to apply for a replacement.
3. Bring your police report and passport to the appointment.
There is a fee to replace a lost card (usually similar to the original application fee).

EU BLUE CARD (also an eAT):
The EU Blue Card is a type of residence permit for highly-skilled workers and is also issued as an eAT card. It has the words "EU Blue Card" printed on it.

DIFFERENCE BETWEEN RESIDENCE PERMIT AND VISA:
- A visa is a sticker in your passport and is temporary.
- A residence permit (eAT) is the plastic card and is the official long-term permission to stay.
- When you first arrive in Germany with a national visa, you must convert it to a residence permit (eAT) at the Foreigners' Office within the validity of your visa.

FOREIGNERS' OFFICE (AUSLÄNDERBEHÖRDE):
This is the office that handles all residence permit matters for non-EU nationals. Every major city and district has one. Find yours at: bamf.de (Ausländerbehörde locator).

ELECTRONIC RESIDENCE PERMIT (eRP) FOR EU CITIZENS IN GERMANY:
EU citizens registered in Germany can request a declaratory residence document — but this is optional for most EU nationals, as they do not legally require a residence permit. Ask your local Bürgeramt or Ausländerbehörde for details.
